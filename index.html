Writing Notes

  • Title: Needs to be attention-grabbing, predict the content, and be relevant. Use a phrase starting with a gerund (verb + -ing) or a question word.
  • Introduction: Offer information to let readers know what to expect. Limit to around three sentences.
  • Headings: Place before each body paragraph to separate content. Keep them short, specific, and consistent in grammatical structure.
  • Body Paragraphs: Provide relevant information and examples to support what is mentioned under each heading.
  • Conclusion: Wrap up main ideas with a call to action.

Adjusting to Secondary School Life Have you ever felt nervous about stepping into a completely new school environment? When I first became a Form 1 student a few years ago, I was very worried. I did not know anyone, and the campus seemed incredibly huge. However, there is no need to be afraid! As the chairperson of Peer Counsellors, I want to share my experience to reassure you that your secondary school journey will be wonderful. Common Challenges It is completely normal to feel a bit lost at first. You may find it difficult to handle the heavy workload. In primary school, you had fewer subjects to study. Now, revising for so many different subjects can be quite annoying, especially when you have a lot of homework to finish every night. Moreover, meeting new classmates can make some students feel shy and lonely. Useful Advice It sounds like you’re going through a really tough time with all these new changes, but help is always here! For dealing with the situation, it is important to plan your time properly. If I were you, I would write down all the homework and tests in a daily planner. As a result, you will not forget your tasks. One of the suggested methods, which you can try, is to join extra-curricular activities. If you join a sports team or an art club, try to chat with the members there so that you can make new friends who share your interests. In conclusion, becoming a new Form 1 student is a big step, but you are never alone. Be brave, ask for help when you need it, and enjoy your exciting new school life!

Writing Notes

Three Main Parts:

  • Opening Paragraph: Show that you understand the recipient's situation and feelings.
  • Body Paragraphs: Give specific advice to address the situation. Include one main idea in each paragraph clearly shown in the topic sentences. Give reasons and examples to support your advice.
  • Closing Paragraph: Briefly summarise your suggestions and encourage the recipient to try them out.

Dear Winnie, I was very sorry to hear that you have been so upset recently. I understand that you want to drop out of school to pursue a career as a pop singer, but your parents are not letting you do so. It sounds like you are going through a really tough time, and I would like to offer you some advice. First of all, the main challenge that you may face is that the music industry is extremely competitive, and dropping out of school now means you lose a vital backup plan. It is clear that there is a drawback with leaving school early, as you won't have the diploma you need if your singing career doesn't work well. Therefore, I strongly advise you to finish your secondary education first to secure your future. Secondly, if you still want to pursue your dream, it is highly recommended that you should search for part-time jobs in the music industry to contribute to your career knowledge. For example, you can take vocal classes on weekends or join local singing contests. This will allow you to gain stage experience without affecting your daily schoolwork. Finally, you need to communicate peacefully with your parents. A practical solution to this problem would be to ask your parents if you can spend every evening after homework performing live streams to build your audience. If you can prove to them that you can balance both your academic results and your hobby, they will definitely be more supportive of your singing career. To summarize, I suggest that you stay in school, join part-time singing activities, and discuss a fair timetable with your parents. Please do not give up on your dreams, but remember that having a good education is equally important. I hope things get better for you soon! Love, Chris
report_problem Letter of Complaint expand_more

Writing Notes

  • Purpose: A formal letter written to express dissatisfaction and ask for a follow-up action.
  • Tone: Firm and polite.
  • Style Restrictions: You should NOT use:
    • Contractions (e.g., use "I am" instead of "I'm")
    • Abbreviations (e.g., use "Hong Kong" instead of "HK")
    • Direct speech
    • Informal punctuation (e.g., dashes, brackets)
  • Structure: Greeting → Subject → Reason for writing → Complaint(s) → Ask for action → Closing → Sender's signature → Sender's name.

Dear Sir/Madam, I am writing to express my strong dissatisfaction regarding the English tutorial class I attended at your centre with my friend on 3rd March. First of all, the class size was highly misleading. Contrary to what was advertised in your leaflet, the actual learning environment was extremely crowded. The leaflet promised a small interactive class of five students, but there were over twenty students in the room. As a result, it was so noisy that we could hardly hear the tutor. Secondly, the attitude of the staff was completely unacceptable. When I politely asked the receptionist to adjust the air-conditioning because the room was very stuffy, she ignored my request and replied very rudely. These problems seriously affected our learning experience. I look forward to your prompt investigation into these issues and a solution that addresses my concerns. Yours faithfully, Chris Wong
campaign Promotional Speech expand_more

Writing Notes

  • Audience & Occasion: Tailor your content and tone to your audience (e.g., schoolmates) and adapt to the occasion (e.g., morning assembly).
  • Opening Statement: Grab attention, get them engaged, and let them know the purpose. Use a provocative question, relevant quote, brief personal story, or surprising fact.
  • Structure: Add topic sentences at the beginning of every point. Use repetition of keywords/themes to reinforce ideas.
  • End Memorably: Return to the strong purpose and end in a meaningful way (promoting the event, stating how they can help, or sharing a true story).

edit_note Blog Entry expand_more

Writing Notes

  • Content: The whole blog entry centres around your experience in one inter-school competition only. Maintain a balance of content and ensure you respond to every part of the prompt.
  • Organization: Include an attention-grabbing title. Write a summary statement in the opening paragraph which draws readers' attention. End with an overall thought and use the HHH structure to show encouragement.
  • Language focus: Use simple present and past tenses correctly. Use a suitable language style and tone. Ensure pronouns are used consistently (e.g. do not mix you/they when referring to the same group). Incorporate relevant SDT (Show, Don't Tell) phrases to describe the experience.

article Sentence patterns used in a feature article expand_more

Describing the problem

You may find it difficult to …

E.g. You may find it difficult to keep practicing for four days a week.

E.g. You may find it difficult to wake up at 6:30 every morning.

…(n.)… can be quite annoying, especially when …SVO…

E.g. Practicing for four days a week can be quite annoying, especially when you don’t like what you’re doing.

E.g. Waking up at 6:30 every morning can be quite annoying, especially when you had a long night.

Showing empathy

It sounds like you’re going through a really tough time.

That sounds incredibly frustrating.

Giving suggestions

If I were you, I would …(v.)…

E.g. If I were you, I would talk to my coach seriously.

E.g. If I were you, I would sleep earlier the night before.

For dealing with the situation, it is important to …(v.)…

E.g. For dealing with the situation, it is important to let your coach know about your difficulty.

E.g. For dealing with the situation, it is important to set a timetable.

One of the suggested methods, which you can try, is to …(v.)…

E.g. One of the suggested methods, which you can try, is to reduce the number of practices.

edit_note Sentence patterns used in a blog entry expand_more

When things go well

On fire / In sync / Seamless teamwork

E.g. Our main shooter was on fire, hitting every basket.

E.g. We were perfectly in sync during the first half.

E.g. Our passing was fast and showed seamless teamwork.

When things go bad

Under pressure / Slip away / Narrow margin / Unforced errors

E.g. We struggled to play well under the intense pressure.

E.g. We watched the lead slowly slip away from us.

E.g. We lost by a narrow margin (only 1 or 2 points).

E.g. We made too many unforced errors.

Idioms and Expressions

Leave it all on the court / Pick ourselves up / A bitter pill to swallow

E.g. Even though we lost, we can proudly say we left it all on the court.

E.g. It was a bitter pill to swallow, but we will pick ourselves up.
